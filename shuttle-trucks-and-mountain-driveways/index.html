Steep driveways and shuttle trucks

The last mile decides whether the big truck is the right truck.

The truck that fits a suburban cul-de-sac may not fit a switchback, and the one that fits the switchback may not hold your furniture. Crews solve it with a shuttle: they load at the origin in the big truck, park where it can safely stand, and ferry goods in a smaller vehicle for the last stretch. It adds hours and, usually, a charge.

Questions to ask before you book

  1. Describe the road honestly. Grade, width, switchbacks, gravel or paved, whether it is plowed, and whether there is a place to turn around.
  2. Ask who decides. The driver has final say on whether the big truck goes up. Ask what happens, and what it costs, if the driver says no on arrival.
  3. Get the shuttle terms in writing. Is it a flat fee, hourly or per trip? Is the ferry time counted in the estimate or billed on top?
  4. Confirm access in season. Spring mud, winter plowing and HOA gates can each stop a truck.
  5. Send photos. A picture of the driveway and the turnaround is worth more than a paragraph.
If you are driving a rental yourself: a loaded truck is heavy, brakes heat on long descents and low gears do the work. Read the rental company’s mountain-driving guidance, and do not assume a move date that ignores weather.

Prepare the property

  • Clear the route of vehicles, bins and snow berms the day before.
  • Mark the path with something visible; a crew arriving in fog will thank you.
  • Put sand or traction material down if ice is likely. Do not assume the crew carries it.
  • Tell the crew about stairs, decks and any steps with no railing.
  • Keep pets inside and the gate latched.

What the estimate should say

For an in-state move, the Colorado Public Utilities Commission regulates household-goods movers. Ask for a written estimate that lists every line item, including shuttle and long-carry charges, and check the mover’s PUC permit on the commission’s site (puc.colorado.gov). Movers must show their permit number in advertising.

We have not published a price range for shuttles because we have not found an official source for one. Treat any figure you see in a web article as marketing until a written estimate backs it.
