Before you pick a town

  • Drive the route in the season you will live there. A July test drive tells you nothing about January.
  • Ask the HOA or town about truck access, parking and move-in windows. Many have rules; we did not find a statewide standard.
  • Check your emissions status. The Air Care Colorado program covers the Denver metro and North Front Range, so mountain counties are generally not part of it. Verify yours with the county clerk.
  • Know where the nearest mover’s yard is. A crew that starts two hours away may bill travel time. Ask how.

Altitude check

The CDC’s Yellow Book recommends gradual ascent and avoiding alcohol and strenuous exercise for the first two days at higher elevations. For a move into the high country, give the crew and the family a lighter first day if you can (CDC Yellow Book).

Summit County, in one honest paragraph

We did not find a single official source that lists elevations, access rules and move-in limits for every Summit County town, so we are not publishing a table of them. Ask the town hall or HOA directly, and ask your mover whether it has done recent jobs in that exact town.
